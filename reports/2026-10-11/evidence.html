Rule Evidence

Walk-forward test of the Decision Desk rules on 19 years of weekly prices, using only data available at each date.

Following the current rules has not beaten simply holding everything equally on this history: treat the calls as weak evidence.

Over 13 weeks, Accumulate names beat the average instrument by +0.2% and Avoid names by -0.5%. Accumulate minus Avoid t-statistic: -0.12 (non-overlapping samples; ≥2 is conventionally meaningful). No alternative rule passes the adoption test, so the live rule is unchanged.

The Desk as you would run it

Buy on Accumulate at the rule's size (0.5% risk to the exit, capped), hold until the weekly close breaks the exit level (40-week average less 3%), cash otherwise, earning the 13-week T-bill yield, 10 bp cost per trade. The comparison holds the same exposure each week spread equally over every instrument, so the gap is the value of the selection rather than of holding less.

How to use this

  • Supported rules earn more trust. If a stance fails in either half, size it down and lean harder on the checklist.
  • The weekly run re-tests these rules and logs any deterioration in the Improvement Log.

Limits of this test

  • Survivorship: today's instrument list is used for the whole period; past losers that left the list are missing.
  • Price returns in local currency, weekly closes, no taxes or dividends. The Desk simulation charges 10 bp a trade; the other rule tests are before costs.
  • The macro stress overlay (VIX risk budget) is not applied historically.
  • Instruments join the test when their history starts (for example XLC in 2018); each week the rule and the benchmark use the same set of instruments.
  • 83 instruments, 66,155 weekly observations from 2008-01-04: still a small sample for a market regime test.
  • Bad print excluded: LLOY 2007-09-28 closed 1.03902 between 5.22927 and 5.6 (a unit error at the source; the raw file is kept as returned).
  • Bad print excluded: AZN 2007-09-28 closed 3.516 between 24.33 and 25.786 (a unit error at the source; the raw file is kept as returned).
  • Excluded LSEG before 2007-04-20: price level changed 97.2x in one week and stayed there (quote unit or unadjusted corporate action); earlier history dropped
  • Excluded RIO before 2007-06-15: price level changed 112x in one week and stayed there (quote unit or unadjusted corporate action); earlier history dropped
  • Excluded RR. 2020-09-28 to 2020-11-06: Rolls-Royce rights issue (October 2020): IBKR weekly closes jump from 1.13 to 2.19, hold near 2.2-2.4 for three weeks, then fall to 0.70, consistent with a corporate-action adjustment applied part-way through. Not verifiable against a second source in this environment.
